## Deep Dive: Liquid Death — Category Creation via Entertainment Marketing

Liquid Death launched in 2018 with a thesis that sounds absurd: take water — the most commoditized beverage on earth — brand it like a heavy metal record label, sell it in tallboy cans, and build a lifestyle brand. Founder Mike Cessario (former Netflix creative director) understood that distribution was available to anyone; attention was not. Rather than compete on paid media efficiency, Liquid Death built an entertainment engine: darkly comedic video content, celebrity collabs, mockumentaries, and interactive fan campaigns that generate earned media instead of bought reach.

Revenue scaled from **$45M (2021) → $130M (2022) → $333M (2024)**, producing a **$1.4B valuation** by 2024 with projections near $340M in 2026 ([House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/liquid-death-marketing-strategy/)). The output of that strategy: **7M TikTok followers and 7.2M Instagram followers** — the most-followed water brand on both platforms and **14M+ total social followers** across channels ([Fueler.io](https://fueler.io/blog/liquid-death-usage-revenue-valuation-growth-statistics)). Organic impressions at effective $0.50–$0.80 CPM equivalents compress CAC structurally in ways paid media cannot replicate. Retail followed brand equity: Liquid Death expanded to **133K+ store locations — a 731% increase since 2021** — treating shelf presence as brand amplification, not a margin concession. In 2026, the brand is extending into Sparkling Energy, a category extension made viable only because the audience follows the brand, not the product SKU.

> [!tip] Takeaway
> The Liquid Death model works in any commoditized category (supplements, basics, home goods). Define a psychographic identity sharper than your product category, produce entertainment content for that identity, and let audiences self-select before you sell them anything. Entertainment compounds; paid ad spend resets to zero every morning.

## Deep Dive: Dr. Squatch — Viral Video to $1.5B Exit and the CRO Multiplier

Dr. Squatch had been selling natural soap since 2010. In 2018, they released a long-form video — "You're Not a Dish, You're a Man" — combining comedy, education, and product demonstration in a format YouTube treated as content rather than an ad. The video hit **120M views** and drove **30× revenue growth**, scaling from **$4M to $100M+ within two years** ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/dr-squatch-dtc-growth-strategy/)). They didn't stop there. Subsequent campaigns extended the entertainment engine: the Nick Cannon Ball Care campaign generated **11.5 billion earned media impressions and 125 million social interactions** — reach no paid budget could replicate at equivalent cost. Revenue reached an estimated **~$400M by 2024**, with **85% from the DTC website** even after expanding to Target, Walmart, and Costco.

In June 2025, Unilever acquired Dr. Squatch for a reported **$1.5B** — valuing the brand for its DTC model, content-led brand equity, and proven retail expansion capability, not just revenue ([Mintel](https://www.mintel.com/insights/consumer-research/unilever-acquires-dr-squatch-cpg-brand-strategy-analysis/)). What made the unit economics exit-ready: two infrastructure layers built alongside the brand. SplitBase optimized the homepage and bundle flow, adding **+$800K/month in incremental revenue** from existing traffic ([SplitBase](https://splitbase.com/case-studies/dr-squatch)). Northbeam handled measurement — tracking profitable CAC thresholds by channel, flagging "red zones" where spend turned unprofitable, ensuring the brand equity advantage didn't hide deteriorating margins ([Northbeam](https://www.northbeam.io/case-study/profitable-cacs-lifetime-value-and-red-zones-how-dr-squatch-uses-northbeam)). The content created traffic; CRO extracted maximum value from it; measurement kept the P&L clean enough to attract a strategic buyer.

> [!tip] Takeaway
> Three layers make the Dr. Squatch flywheel: **(1)** one or two entertainment-first hero videos per year per category; **(2)** CRO optimization on the bundle and homepage flows that viral traffic hits; **(3)** explicit CAC red zones by channel so brand equity gains don't mask margin erosion. The content builds the brand; the infrastructure builds the business.

| Layer | Dr. Squatch Execution | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Hero content** | "You're Not a Dish" (120M views) | 30× revenue growth |
| **Earned media campaigns** | Nick Cannon Ball Care (11.5B impressions) | Sustained reach at near-zero CPM |
| **CRO optimization** | SplitBase homepage + bundles | +$800K/month incremental revenue |
| **Measurement infrastructure** | Northbeam CAC red zones | Profitable unit economics at scale |
| **Exit** | Unilever acquisition (June 2025) | **$1.5B valuation** |

## Quick Headlines

**AI Conversational Commerce:** Gorgias' [State of Conversational Commerce 2026](https://www.gorgias.com/state-of-conversational-commerce-2026/introduction) (16K+ brands, 400 ecommerce leaders) reports **79% of brands** say AI conversational commerce increased sales; AI-influenced orders grew **273% QoQ** in 2025; but **86% of brands** still see 25%+ of AI interactions escalate to humans. The takeaway: AI is compressing support costs and generating incremental sales, but hybrid AI + human is the current operating standard — not full automation.

**Gymshark at £646M:** Gymshark hit **£646M in revenue for 2025** (6.4% YoY growth) with **96% through owned DTC channels** and a Regent Street flagship opened in 2024 as its hybrid retail anchor. The Gymshark Athlete Program (125+ creators) drives **30%+ of social revenue** — proof that influencer-as-brand-architecture scales before platform ad optimization becomes the primary growth lever ([Modaes Global](https://www.modaes.com/global/companies/gymshark-record-turnover-of-7486-million-euros-in-2025-up-64)).

## Action Items
- [ ] Audit content-to-paid ratio: if 90%+ of acquisition content is paid ads (not owned/earned), unit economics are exposed to platform volatility — build one entertainment-first content series this quarter
- [ ] When was your last hero video designed to generate **organic shares** rather than just paid impressions? Produce one long-form educational + entertaining video per product category per year
- [ ] Run a CRO audit on bundle and homepage flows — Dr. Squatch's +$800K/month came from existing traffic, not new spend
- [ ] Set explicit CAC **red zone thresholds** by channel: the level where you cut spend regardless of in-platform ROAS optics
- [ ] If approaching $50M+ revenue: map the CPG exit playbook — Unilever valued Dr. Squatch for DTC model + brand equity + retail expansion proof, not just top-line revenue
