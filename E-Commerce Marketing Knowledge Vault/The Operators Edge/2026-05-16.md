## Deep Dive: AG1 — The Single-SKU Subscription Machine

AG1 (Athletic Greens) launched in 2010 and sold one product for 14 years: a daily greens powder blend, now called AG1. No line extensions, no seasonal SKUs, no channel diversification until 2025. What looks like product restraint is actually a precision business model — every dollar of customer acquisition cost flows toward one SKU with one retention mechanism: a subscription that auto-ships. Under CEO Kat Cole (promoted 2024), AG1 is finally expanding — projecting **$600M in revenue for 2025** and entering Target as its first mass retail launch — but the base was built entirely DTC ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/01/21/athletic-greens-launching-new-products-channels-2025/); [Retail Brew](https://www.retailbrew.com/stories/2025/01/21/after-14-years-selling-one-product-in-dtc-ag1-is-ready-to-expand)).

The acquisition engine is podcast sponsorship at a scale most brands never contemplate. AG1 is estimated to spend approximately **$2.2M per month on podcast advertising alone** ([Latterly](https://www.latterly.org/ag1-marketing-strategy/)), signing **6–12 month host-read partnerships** with shows like *The Tim Ferriss Show*, *Armchair Expert with Dax Shepard*, and *The Mindset Mentor*. The structural logic is the same as LMNT (covered May 15): host-read ads carry personal endorsement weight that no display or social creative can replicate, and a listener who hears the same host vouch for AG1 across 20+ episodes converts with a trust coefficient a Meta cold audience never reaches. The long-term contracts also create compounding frequency — listeners hear the ad in context, repeatedly, until the purchase decision becomes obvious.

The subscription funnel architecture compounds the acquisition advantage. AG1's first-purchase offer bundles **two free gifts** (a vitamin D3+K2 bottle + AG1 travel packs) with the subscription signup — reducing the perceived risk of a $79+/month commitment. Inside the funnel: multiple subscription frequency options compress the LTV payback period for each acquired customer. The result is a brand that can afford higher podcast CPMs than competitors because it's not calculating CAC against a single transaction — it's calculating against a customer likely to subscribe for 12+ months ([HeyDeva](https://www.heyseva.com/blog-posts/the-ag1-influence-blueprint-how-athletic-greens-scaled-through-creator-partnerships)).

> [!tip] Takeaway
> AG1's podcast model works because **duration beats reach**: six consecutive months on one niche show outperforms a one-month blitz across ten. If you're testing podcast advertising, commit to a 12-episode minimum with a single host before evaluating performance — and lead with a free-gift offer, not a discount code.

## Deep Dive: True Classic — CM ROAS and the Mathematical Scaling Playbook

True Classic launched around 2019-2020 with $3,000 in seed money, no fashion industry background, and a thesis that men would pay a premium for well-fitting basics sold with humor. By 2022 they'd crossed **$150M in DTC revenue, profitably**. By 2025, Shopify profiled them as a **$500M brand**. In April 2025, 1686 Partners led a **Series E investment** with the brand reportedly at a **$700M+ valuation** ([Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/articles/direct-to-consumer/true-classic-raises-money/)). That trajectory — profitable from nearly day one, no institutional capital required to reach $150M — is the result of a measurement framework most DTC brands skip.

True Classic's president Ben Yahalom coined **"CM ROAS"** — contribution-margin ROAS — as the brand's primary paid media efficiency metric. Standard ROAS measures revenue ÷ ad spend. CM ROAS adjusts revenue by subtracting variable costs (COGS, fulfillment, returns, payment processing) before dividing by spend. The result: a number that reflects actual margin generated per dollar of media investment rather than top-line revenue that looks healthy but hides unprofitable volume. True Classic routes the **majority of its Meta budget through Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns**, Meta's AI-powered allocation tool, but targets CM ROAS thresholds rather than platform-native ROAS — which means it's optimizing for profitability that Meta's algorithm would otherwise ignore ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/advertiser/mens-apparel-brand-true-classic-takes-a-mathematical-approach-to-growth-marketing/)).

The creative engine that feeds the funnel is deliberately lean: **$25–$50/day** per concept to test, profits reinvested to scale winners, budget pulled on losers within days. **80% of creative is produced in-house** to maintain speed and brand voice, and the content split is disciplined — **90% direct-response conversion content, 10% brand awareness** ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/true-classic-tshirt-business-content-marketing-strategies); [Retail TouchPoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/topics/digital-commerce/true-classic-t-shirts-tees-digital-marketing-magic-dtc-success)). Humor is a consistent creative lever — ads that make the target customer laugh at themselves ("you don't need to dress up, you just need to fit better") outperform aspirational lifestyle imagery because they meet the customer in actual self-perception rather than projected identity.

> [!tip] Takeaway
> Calculate CM ROAS for every active campaign this week: take reported revenue, subtract COGS + fulfillment + estimated returns + payment fees for those orders, then divide by ad spend. Any campaign where standard ROAS looks strong but CM ROAS is below your floor is consuming margin you don't see in platform dashboards. This is the number that tells you whether to scale or cut.

> [!info] The In-House Creative Flywheel
> True Classic's 80% in-house content production isn't a cost-cutting choice — it's a speed advantage. When a concept tests well at $25/day on Monday, in-house teams can produce five variations by Thursday. Agency timelines make that cycle 2–4 weeks. In a fast-moving paid media environment, **production speed is a media efficiency multiplier**.

## Quick Headlines

**2026 DTC P&L Benchmarks (A2X, 18 brands, 7–9 figures):** Brands with **gross margins above 70%** are far more likely to reach and sustain 8-figure revenue. Below **65% gross margin**, scaling becomes structurally difficult regardless of growth rate. The $10M–$50M cohort faced the worst compounding headwinds in 2025: CAC inflation, tariff-driven COGS pressure, and margin squeeze hitting simultaneously. **Implication: gross margin is a prerequisite for scale, not a downstream benefit of it** ([A2X — 2026 Ecom P&L Benchmark Report](https://www.a2xaccounting.com/ecommerce-accounting-hub/2026-ecom-pl-benchmark-report)).

**Tariff Pricing Reversal (Common Thread Collective):** **71% of DTC brands raised prices** in response to 2025 tariffs — and are now navigating the reversal challenge as tariffs ease. CTC's analysis: brands that raised prices cleanly (one SKU-level adjustment, communicated with a narrative) are holding more of the increase than brands that raised quietly or bundled changes into promotional mechanics. **Lesson: price increases stick when they're explained, not hidden** ([Common Thread Collective](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/tariff-pricing-strategy-dtc-brands-2026)).

## Action Items
- [ ] Calculate **CM ROAS** for your top 5 ad campaigns this week — subtract COGS + fulfillment + returns from attributed revenue before dividing by spend. Pause any campaign below your profitability floor even if platform ROAS looks strong
- [ ] Audit podcast/audio spend: are partnerships **6–12 months with host-read scripts**, or one-off pre-rolls? Negotiate duration and host authenticity before volume
- [ ] If gross margin is below 65%, **prioritize margin expansion before scaling ad spend** — the A2X benchmark data shows scaling below 65% GM compounds losses rather than amortizing them
- [ ] Set an in-house content production target: even moving from 20% to 40% in-house content improves the creative testing cycle speed — reduce agency dependency on iteration, not ideation
- [ ] Review post-tariff pricing: if you raised prices in 2025 and tariffs are now easing, decide explicitly whether to hold or reduce — and communicate it with a narrative, not silence
